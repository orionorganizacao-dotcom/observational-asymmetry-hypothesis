# OAH Falsification Criteria

## Purpose

A theory that cannot fail cannot be tested.

The following conditions define observations that would weaken or falsify the Observational Asymmetry Hypothesis (OAH).

---

## F1 — Reconstruction Equivalence

If systems without Pre-Decisional Commitment consistently reconstruct decisional states with the same reliability and independence as systems with commitment infrastructure, OAH weakens substantially.

Expected OAH prediction:

Commitment-based systems preserve superior evidential integrity.

---

## F2 — Temporal Irrelevance

If preserving decisional states before output produces no measurable gain in auditability, contestability, or reconstruction fidelity, the temporal premise of OAH weakens.

Expected OAH prediction:

Earlier preservation improves independent reconstruction.

---

## F3 — Observer Independence Irrelevance

If independent observers provide no advantage over self-reported reconstruction mechanisms, the asymmetry condition weakens.

Expected OAH prediction:

Independent evidence outperforms self-generated explanations.

---

## F4 — Complete Retroactive Recovery

If decisional states can be reconstructed perfectly after execution without prior preservation, OAH Type I becomes invalid.

Expected OAH prediction:

Lost decisional states remain unrecoverable.

---

## Status

Current state:

No known evidence falsifies OAH.
